# Onboarding interview

Goal: enough signal to write `profile.md` and `rubric.md` without guessing, and the facts (current role, real numbers) that turn the CV from reformatted into improved. Read `cv/source.md` first. Ask one question at a time, in the workspace language, and accept short answers. Do not ask what the CV already answers; confirm it in one line instead ("Your CV says you have been remote since 2021, correct?").

## Keep the answers

Write every answer to `<workspace>/interview.md` as soon as it arrives (`## <question>` then the answer, in the user's words). If that file already exists when the interview starts, read it, say which questions are answered, and continue from the first one without an answer. Never ask the same thing twice.

## Opening confirmation (before question 1)

One line, not a question of its own: "Your CV ends with <last role> in <month year>. What has changed since then?" New jobs, freelance work, projects and certifications from that answer go into the CV; this is the most common gap in an old CV. In the same message, confirm the contact line the CV needs (email, phone, LinkedIn URL, portfolio) and ask only for what is missing.

## Fixed questions (always, in this order)

1. **Target roles.** Which role titles are you going for? Name the ones you would take tomorrow and the ones you would consider.
2. **Roles to avoid.** Which titles or kinds of work do you not want, even if the match looks good?
3. **Location and remote.** Where do you live, which time zones can you cover, and is hybrid or on-site acceptable anywhere?
4. **Work authorization.** Where can you legally work as an employee, and are you open to contractor arrangements elsewhere?
5. **Compensation.** What is your target and your hard floor (say the period: month or year, and the currency)?
6. **Dealbreakers.** Company types, industries, or practices you refuse (crypto, gambling, agencies, unpaid trials, …).
7. **Skills the CV undersells.** Anything you are good at that the CV does not show, and anything on the CV you no longer want to do.
8. **Company stage and domain.** Startup, scale-up, enterprise? Which product domains excite you most, and which are fine but not a goal?

## Extra questions (at most 5, only when the CV leaves a real gap)

Ask only if you cannot fill a profile section or write strong CV bullets after the eight above. Ask these first, because they improve the CV the most:

- **Numbers for recent roles.** For each of the two or three most recent roles whose CV bullets carry no number, ask for one or two real metrics: users, revenue, time or cost saved, volume handled, team size, before-and-after. Offer examples from their own bullets ("You wrote 'automated onboarding': how many customers per month, or how many hours did it save?"). If they do not know, keep the bullet without a number. Never invent or estimate one for them.
- **Current work missing from the CV**, if the opening confirmation surfaced something without enough detail to write it.

Other typical gaps:

- Years of experience are unclear (overlapping dates, freelancing).
- Seniority target (IC vs lead vs manager).
- Languages and level, if the CV does not state them.
- Proof of remote or async work, if the CV never mentions it.
- A skill cluster you cannot tag (Expert vs Exposure) because the CV only name-drops it.
- Notice period or earliest start date, if the user mentioned urgency.

## Closing

Summarize the profile in five lines, ask for one correction round, then write `profile.md` and `rubric.md`. Tell the user they can change either file by hand at any time, and that `interview.md` keeps their answers for later updates.
